Finding a Personal Style:

An Interview with Caden Lee

1. Getting Into Graphic Design

Caden I've always been interested in fine arts. Since I was a kid, I worked with traditional materials, and throughout high school, too. I only started learning graphic design during my last year of high school. I wasn't really sure what I wanted to do, but I was always into charcoal, colored pencils, and painting.

I talked to my art teacher a lot, and she encouraged me to try graphic design and told me about the program at CalArts. I started learning Illustrator and Photoshop during my last year of high school, just a few weeks before my portfolio was due. I applied to graphic design here, and I got in.

It was an intense process, but after my first year, everything started to click. I became more interested in graphic design, and by my third year, I started exploring motion graphics as well

2. Returning to Traditional Materials

Michael You mentioned working with charcoal and other traditional mediums. Do you see those materials incorporated into your work now?

Caden For the last two years, I got so into digital art that I completely stopped working traditionally. I didn't forget my fine arts skills, but I definitely stopped using them. Recently, I wanted to switch up my style and explore a little more. I did a project using analog materials I had worked with when I was focused on fine arts and incorporated them into graphic design to make a large-scale abstract piece. That was really fun.

3. Favorite Projects

Michael What's a project you've worked on that you're especially proud of, and why?

Caden I'm especially proud of the motion work I did last year. I had the opportunity to explore motion graphics more, and I took a motion class at CalArts where I got to make a lot of cool things. It helped me explore my skills and find my own style. I also made a compilation video of all my motion work.

Another project I'm proud of is Rogue, a shoe-branding project I did during my third year. I really love fashion and branding, so working on something I was interested in made the process enjoyable. I got to explore my style in that way. The project was about having weapons concealed inside shoes so women could defend themselves. It was really fun to design.

Michael What was the motion work like for Rogue?

Caden The project was fun because it helped me explore the idea of concealing something dangerous behind a high-fashion, sophisticated, and elegant brand. I wanted to show that something dangerous was hidden beneath the surface, even if you couldn't tell at first. I used a lot of glitches and effects over videos of the shoes.

Michael Do you have a favorite effect in After Effects that you like using?

Caden I love time displacement. I think that's what it's called. It takes frames from the seconds before and overlays them onto the current image, which creates a really cool effect.

4. Finding Inspiration

Michael Where do you find inspiration for your work?

Caden I find a lot of inspiration in things I already really love. I have a specific interest in fashion because I wanted to go into fashion design before graphic design. I love looking at old runway shows, experimental pieces, and what fashion designers are doing today, as well as what designers did in the past.

I also find inspiration in nature and unusual organic forms. I had a phase when I was really interested in bacteria and organic forms. A lot of my friends in high school studied science, so they would send me pictures of microscopic organisms moving under a microscope. I incorporate a lot of those strange forms into my work, too.

5. Life Outside of Design

Michael What are some of your favorite hobbies? What do you like to do outside of design?

Caden I love doing different things. I feel like it's so important to have a life outside of work, or else you literally lose your whole identity. On my own, I love crocheting and making art. I like working with traditional materials and making pieces for myself outside of the computer.

But mostly, I love going out and exploring LA, going to concerts and shows, getting dressed up, and spending time with my friends and girlfriend. I enjoy listening to good music, going to jazz shows, and eating at good restaurants. Right now, I really love shabu and hot pot.

6. Queer Culture and Creative Influence

Michael How, if at all, do you think your background has shaped your current design style?

Caden Being queer has influenced my work a lot, especially through music and the artists I look at for inspiration. A lot of the music I listen to comes from queer artists, and music has a big influence on my work. I'm currently doing a project in my motion class about queer artists in techno. I'm making a motion montage video, and it's an important topic to me because a lot of queer culture gets erased from music, especially when it's commercialized through big venues and concerts.

Techno has roots in queer culture. It was a space where people could come together and dance to whatever they wanted. I want to highlight that history in my video and talk about the culture behind the music we know today.

7. Cultural Identity and Design

Michael Do you think being Korean has influenced your design in any way? Do you see that incorporated into your work, or would you like to explore it?

Caden I haven't really incorporated Korean elements into my work. I do follow Korean designers and look at what they're doing, though. I'm Korean American, and I grew up here, so my relationship with my cultural background is something I think about. I don't know if it necessarily matters to every project, but I can relate to looking at design through an Asian American lens.

From traditional fine arts to digital experimentation, Caden's creative practice continues to evolve through the things they love. Fashion, unusual natural forms, motion graphics, and queer music culture all inform their work in different ways. Outside the studio, Caden makes time for personal art, crocheting, music, and relationships—interests that help them maintain an identity beyond design.
